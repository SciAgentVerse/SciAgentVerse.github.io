Overview

The First Workshop on Scientific Agents in the Digital World brings together researchers developing agents that can formulate scientific questions, carry out computational investigations, and revise their conclusions in light of evidence. We focus on scientific inquiry conducted through digital environments, where agents interact with scientific knowledge and software to pursue a research objective: exploring candidate explanations, running controlled computational tests, and examining results before committing to further investigation.

Across domains such as materials science, chemistry, physics, biology, and social science, we seek to understand how agents can produce findings that are scientifically meaningful and independently verifiable. We welcome both methodological contributions and concrete scientific applications.

Topics of interest

Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Scientific knowledge and hypothesis generation. Methods for formulating testable hypotheses from scientific literature and domain knowledge, including identifying research gaps, reconciling conflicting findings, and using evidence to assess novelty and refine or reject hypotheses.
  • Simulation and experimental planning. Agent approaches to selecting informative tests under resource constraints, using scientific simulators and learned predictive models to guide exploration across scientific domains, including balancing candidate investigation with testing uncertain assumptions and determining where further validation is needed.
  • Scientific workflow execution and memory. Systems that execute research workflows through scientific software while maintaining hypotheses, modeling decisions, and intermediate results; diagnosing failed runs and revising workflows while preserving scientific context and decision rationale.
  • Learning and adaptation for scientific investigation. Approaches that improve agent capabilities through demonstrations, interaction, and feedback, including foundation model adaptation, learning from unsuccessful attempts, continual learning, and transferring knowledge and skills across tasks, agent tools, and environments.
  • Collaborative scientific inquiry. Collaboration among specialized agents and researchers to coordinate expertise, examine hypotheses, and resolve conflicting interpretations, with attention to effective human intervention and clear responsibility for scientific decisions.
  • Evaluation and trustworthiness of scientific agents. Evaluation of scientific validity and reproducibility through traceable evidence and independent scrutiny of reported findings.

Submission guidelines

We welcome research papers, position papers, work in progress, and demonstrations.

Format

  • Up to 8 pages for the initial submission, plus unlimited pages for references and supplementary materials.
  • Manuscripts must be submitted in PDF using the ACM conference template (sigconf), as used by WSDM 2027.

Review

  • Reviewing is double-blind. Submissions that are not properly anonymized will be desk-rejected.
  • Each accepted paper will be presented as an oral talk (8 min + 2 min Q&A) and/or a poster.

Non-archival

  • The workshop is non-archival. Accepted papers will be posted on this website but will not be included in any proceedings.

Presentation & open science

  • WSDM 2027 is an in-person event; at least one author of each accepted paper should present on site in Hong Kong.
  • We encourage sharing code, datasets, simulation configurations, workflow descriptions, and evaluation resources where possible.
