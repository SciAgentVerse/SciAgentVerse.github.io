About the workshop

Scientific discovery proceeds through repeated cycles of gathering evidence, proposing hypotheses, and testing them against observations. Scientific agents offer a way to coordinate these steps — connecting scientific literature and databases with computational analysis, simulation, and experimental validation, and using each result to guide the next investigation.

This workshop focuses on scientific inquiry conducted through digital environments, where agents interact with scientific knowledge and software to pursue a research objective. These environments let agents explore candidate explanations, conduct controlled computational tests, and examine results before committing to further investigation. Across materials science, chemistry, physics, biology, and social science, we ask how agents can connect scientific reasoning with executable tests, reason about the limits of simulators and learned models, respond to unexpected observations, and preserve the rationale behind earlier decisions in multi-stage investigations.

How can agents use digital environments to produce findings that are scientifically meaningful and independently verifiable?

1

Evidence to hypotheses

How should agents combine literature, structured scientific data, and domain knowledge to formulate hypotheses with traceable evidence?

2

Planning under uncertainty

How should agents plan retrieval, simulations, and experiments under uncertainty and resource constraints — and decide when a hypothesis needs revision?

3

Evaluating discovery

How should evaluation connect the quality of individual agent decisions with scientific validity, novelty, reproducibility, and discovery efficiency?

Scope & topics

We welcome methodological contributions and concrete scientific applications, including but not limited to:

Scientific knowledge & hypothesis generation

Formulating testable hypotheses from literature and domain knowledge: identifying research gaps, reconciling conflicting findings, and assessing novelty.

Simulation & experimental planning

Selecting informative tests under resource constraints, using scientific simulators and learned predictive models to guide exploration.

Scientific workflow execution & memory

Executing research workflows through scientific software while maintaining hypotheses, modeling decisions, intermediate results, and decision rationale.

Learning & adaptation

Improving agents through demonstrations, interaction, and feedback: foundation model adaptation, learning from failures, and continual transfer across tasks and tools.

Collaborative scientific inquiry

Specialized agents and researchers coordinating expertise, examining hypotheses, and resolving conflicting interpretations with clear human responsibility.

Evaluation & trustworthiness

Assessing scientific validity and reproducibility through traceable evidence and independent scrutiny of reported findings.

Why WSDM?

Scientific agents bring a distinctive setting to WSDM's research on information access: retrieved knowledge becomes part of an evolving scientific investigation. Its value depends on how it informs a hypothesis, motivates a computational test, or helps interpret an unexpected result. As new observations challenge earlier assumptions, they change what evidence the agent needs and which candidates should be prioritized.

Search and information retrieval connect agents with scientific literature and open repositories; knowledge graphs and data mining help organize heterogeneous evidence and analyze simulation and experimental results. The workshop brings together the agent, information access, and AI-for-Science communities with domain scientists to develop a shared research agenda around these feedback-driven discovery workflows.
